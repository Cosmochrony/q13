  \begin{abstract}
    On a supplied Riemannian metric, the companion Gravity paper states the conditions under which the Einstein
    tensor is the locally dominant horizontal $a_2$ response (a non-zero matched Einstein coefficient and small curvature
    relative to the renormalized higher-derivative scale), and the companion Q12 paper derives the local
    Yang--Mills equations as the vertical $a_4$ response
    of the projective spectral entropy functional
    $S_\Pi[g,A] = \tfrac{1}{2}\log\det' \mathcal{A}_{g,A}$.
    The present paper joins the two variations within a single renormalized local action and
    establishes a \emph{conditional} Einstein--Yang--Mills system.
    Varying the matched action with respect to the connection at fixed metric gives
    $D_\mu F^{a\mu\nu} = 0$; varying the same $\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F^2)$ term with respect to the
    metric gives its energy--momentum tensor, through
    $(1/\sqrt{g})\,\delta\!\int\!\sqrt{g}\,\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F^2)/\delta g^{\mu\nu}
     = 2\tau_{\mu\nu}$ with
    $\tau_{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{tr}_\rho(F_{\mu\rho}F_\nu{}^\rho - \tfrac14 g_{\mu\nu}F^2)$.
    Since $\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F^2) = I_\rho\,F^a_{\mu\nu}F^{a\,\mu\nu}$, canonical matching fixes
    the products $c_{\mathrm{EH}} = 1/(16\pi G_N)$ and
    $c_F I_\rho = 1/(4g_{\mathrm{YM}}^2)$, where $I_\rho$ is the Dynkin index of the fibre
    representation; the Einstein equation with Yang--Mills source then holds \emph{by definition}
    of $T^{\mathrm{YM}}$, with $8\pi G_N = 1/(2c_{\mathrm{EH}})$ depending on the Einstein
    coefficient alone.
    Nothing here predicts a coupling: $G_N$, $g_{\mathrm{YM}}$ and the cosmological coefficient
    $\Lambda_{\mathrm{ren}}$, which does contribute to the metric equation, are independent
    renormalized matching data.
    The derivation is Euclidean, and identifying the result with the Lorentzian
    Einstein--Yang--Mills equations requires a continuation not performed here.
    For the $a_6$ sector we give only a representative spanning list of the invariants that can
    occur, modulo integrations by parts and Bianchi identities: in four dimensions $a_6$ produces
    no ultraviolet divergence, and the renormalized dimension-six coefficients are themselves
    matching data.
    Statements about $\Lambda^4$, $\Lambda^2$ and $\log\Lambda$ behaviour refer specifically to a
    proper-time cutoff and are not scheme-independent.
    The gauge group $G_\Pi$ is a supplied compact group: the gauge-structure sub-programme does
    not identify it, and no result below depends on which group is supplied.
    \emph{Interpretive status.} The reading of gravity and gauge dynamics as \emph{stratified by
    Seeley--DeWitt order} rather than unified by an enlarged symmetry group is offered as an
    interpretive outlook, not a theorem.
  \end{abstract}

  \textbf{Keywords:} gauge--gravity synthesis, Einstein--Yang--Mills equations,
  Seeley--DeWitt expansion, spectral entropy functional, Yang--Mills stress tensor,
  renormalization matching, non-injective projection, admissible bundle.

  \section{Introduction}

    \subsection{Context}
      \label{ssec:context}

      The Cosmochrony framework derives emergent physics from a generally non-injective
      projection $\Pi\colon\chi\to\mathcal{M}$ of a static relational substrate $\chi$.
      Within this framework, the Foundation paper sets out the admissibility structure
      of $\Pi$~\cite{Beau2026m}, and the Q-series derives spectral consequences of that
      structure~\cite{Beau2026q1,Beau2026q2}.
      The spectral entropy functional
      \[
        S_\Pi[g] = \tfrac{1}{2}\log\det'\ \mathcal{A}_g,
      \]
      where $\mathcal{A}_g = -(\nabla^g)^2$ is the Laplace-type operator of a supplied Riemannian metric, is the
      functional of the Gravity paper; the identification of $\mathcal{A}_g$ with an operator constructed from the
      admissibility data is not established.
      Its metric variation contains the Einstein tensor through the $a_2$ Seeley--DeWitt coefficient, as the
      infrared-dominant local response when the matched Einstein coefficient is non-zero and the curvature is small
      relative to the renormalized higher-derivative scale~\cite{Beau2026h}.

      The Q12 paper~\cite{Beau2026q12} extended the functional to the gauge sector
      by coupling $\mathcal{A}_g$ to an admissible connection $A$ on the associated
      vector bundle:
      \[
        S_\Pi[g,A] = \tfrac{1}{2}\log\det'\ \mathcal{A}_{g,A},
        \qquad
        \mathcal{A}_{g,A} = -(\nabla^A)^2 + E.
      \]
      The vertical variation $\delta_A S_\Pi = 0$ then yields the Yang--Mills equations
      $D_\mu F^{a\mu\nu} = 0$ through the $a_4$ coefficient.
      The two results place gravity and Yang--Mills dynamics at different levels of
      the Seeley--DeWitt hierarchy of the \emph{same} functional, separated by geometric
      direction (horizontal versus vertical variation) and by spectral order.

    \subsection{Main results}
      \label{ssec:main}

      The present paper delivers two things:

      \begin{enumerate}
        \item \textbf{A conditional joint variational result.}
        Varying a single renormalized local action with respect to both $g$ and $A$ gives the
        coupled Einstein--Yang--Mills system, the Yang--Mills energy--momentum tensor arising
        from the metric dependence of the matched $\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F^2)$ term
        (Theorem~\ref{thm:EYM}). The normalisation is fixed by canonical matching, not predicted:
        $G_N$, $g_{\mathrm{YM}}$ and $\Lambda_{\mathrm{ren}}$ are independent renormalized data
        (Remark~\ref{rem:no-prediction}), and the result is stated in Euclidean signature
        (Remark~\ref{rem:signature}).

        \item \textbf{An inventory of the $a_6$ sector.}
        We give a representative spanning list of the gauge--gravity invariants that can occur at
        order $a_6$, modulo integrations by parts and Bianchi identities, and delimit what is
        \emph{not} determined: in four dimensions $a_6$ produces no ultraviolet divergence, the
        renormalized dimension-six coefficients are matching data, and no unique cross-term is
        singled out (Section~\ref{sec:a6}).
      \end{enumerate}

      No non-linear completion of the joint functional and no numerical value for the ratio
      $G_N g_{\mathrm{YM}}^2$ are claimed; both are listed among the open points
      (Section~\ref{ssec:open}).

  \section{The joint spectral entropy functional}
    \label{sec:joint}

    \subsection{The admissible principal bundle}
      \label{ssec:bundle}

      Following Q12~\cite{Beau2026q12}, let $(M,g)$ be a compact Riemannian four-manifold
      and let $P\to M$ be a principal $G_\Pi$-bundle, where $G_\Pi$ is a compact gauge group
      supplied as an input and not fixed here (Section~\ref{ssec:Hcolor}).
      Let $E = P\times_\rho V$ be the associated vector bundle for a representation
      $\rho\colon G_\Pi\to \mathrm{GL}(V)$.
      An admissible connection $A\in\mathcal{A}(P)$ defines a covariant derivative
      $\nabla^A$ on $E$ and a curvature two-form $F_A = dA + A\wedge A\in\Omega^2(M,\mathrm{ad}\,P)$.

    \subsection{The Laplace-type operator and its decomposition}
      \label{ssec:Laplace}

      Define the Laplace-type operator on sections of $E$:
      \[
        \mathcal{A}_{g,A} = -(\nabla^A)^2 + E,
      \]
      where $E\in\Gamma(\mathrm{End}\,E)$ is the endomorphism determined by the admissibility
      structure.
      Lemma~1 of Q12~\cite{Beau2026q12} states that under minimal coupling the coefficient $a_2$
      is independent of $A$, so that the \emph{vertical} variation at fixed metric isolates the
      Yang--Mills sector.
      It does \emph{not} assert a full horizontal--vertical decoupling: the metric variation of
      the $a_4$ gauge term is precisely the Yang--Mills stress tensor, an $a_4$-order
      back-reaction which is the subject of Section~\ref{sec:EYM}.
      No operator split of $\mathcal{A}_{g,A}$ into horizontal, vertical and mixed pieces is used
      anywhere below.
      The spectral entropy functional is then:
      \[
        S_\Pi[g,A] = \tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}\log\mathcal{A}_{g,A}
        \;=\;
        \tfrac{1}{2}\log\det'\,\mathcal{A}_{g,A}.
      \]
      This is the common generating functional for both the gravitational and gauge sectors.

    \subsection{Seeley--DeWitt expansion of the joint functional}
      \label{ssec:SDW}

      We adopt throughout the convention of Q12~\cite{Beau2026q12}, in which the factor
      $(4\pi)^{-2}$ sits once in the heat-kernel prefactor and is not repeated inside any
      coefficient:
      \begin{equation}
        \mathrm{Tr}\,e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{g,A}}
        \;\sim\;
        (4\pi t)^{-2}\sum_{k=0}^\infty a_{2k}[\mathcal{A}_{g,A}]\,t^{k}
        \quad (t\to 0^+),
        \label{eq:heat-kernel}
      \end{equation}
      where the Seeley--DeWitt coefficients $a_{2k}$ are local integrals of
      $g$, $A$, and their covariant derivatives:
      \begin{align}
        a_0 &= \int_M \mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{Id}_V)\,\sqrt{g}\,d^4x,
        \label{eq:a0}
        \\[4pt]
        a_2[g]
        &= \int_M \frac{R}{6}\,\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{Id}_V)\,\sqrt{g}\,d^4x,
        \label{eq:a2}
        \\[4pt]
        a_4[g,A]
        &= \int_M
        \Bigl[
          \alpha_1 R^2 + \alpha_2 R_{\mu\nu}R^{\mu\nu}
          + \alpha_3 R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
          + \tfrac{1}{12}\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu})
          \Bigr]\sqrt{g}\,d^4x,
        \label{eq:a4}
      \end{align}
      with $\mathrm{tr}_\rho$ the trace in the fibre representation $\rho$, the gauge component
      $\Omega^{\mathrm{gauge}}_{\mu\nu} = F_{\mu\nu}$ isolated from the total bundle curvature as
      in Q12~\cite{Beau2026q12} (the total $\Omega$ also carries the spin-connection curvature,
      which feeds the purely gravitational invariants), and $\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3$
      numerical coefficients~\cite{Vassilevich2003}.
      The term $\tfrac{1}{12}\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F^2)$ is the unique gauge-invariant $a_4$
      contribution quadratic in the gauge curvature; its normalisation depends on the
      representation through the Dynkin index $I_\rho$ and is therefore not universal.

      \paragraph{Regularization scheme.}
      The two schemes used in this programme must not be conflated~\cite{Beau2026h}.
      In a \emph{proper-time cutoff} at scale $\Lambda$, the small-$t$ region generates power
      divergences and the functional organises as
      \begin{align}
        S_\Pi[g,A]
        &= c_{(0)}\Lambda^4 a_0
        \;+\;
        c_{(2)}\Lambda^2 a_2[g]
        \;+\;
        c_{(4)}\log\frac{\Lambda}{\mu}\,a_4[g,A]
        \;+\;
        S_\Pi^{\mathrm{fin}}[g,A]
        \;+\;
        O\!\left(\Lambda^{-2}\right),
        \label{eq:SDW-expansion}
      \end{align}
      with $S_\Pi^{\mathrm{fin}}$ the finite part at the renormalization scale $\mu$.
      By contrast, the zeta-regularized determinant carries no power divergences at all: it
      returns a finite result together with the $\log\mu$ ambiguity governed by $\zeta_A(0)=a_4$.
      Equation~\eqref{eq:SDW-expansion}, and every statement below about $\Lambda^4$, $\Lambda^2$
      or $\log\Lambda$ behaviour, therefore belongs specifically to the proper-time scheme and is
      \emph{not} scheme-independent.
      What is common to both schemes is only that the gauge sector enters at $a_4$ and the
      Einstein sector at $a_2$.

      The $a_0$ sector contributes to the equations of motion: since
      $\delta\sqrt{g}/\delta g^{\mu\nu} = -\tfrac12\sqrt{g}\,g_{\mu\nu}$, it yields a term
      proportional to $g_{\mu\nu}$ in the metric equation.
      Its renormalized coefficient is a matching datum, written $\Lambda_{\mathrm{ren}}$ below.

  \section{Joint Einstein--Yang--Mills equations of motion}
    \label{sec:EYM}

    \subsection{Metric variation of the joint functional}
      \label{ssec:metric-var}

      We work throughout with a single renormalized local action, in which each surviving
      invariant carries its own Wilson coefficient fixed by matching:
      \begin{equation}
        S^{\mathrm{ren}}[g,A]
        \;=\;
        \int_M \Bigl[
          -\,\Lambda_{\mathrm{ren}}
          \;+\; c_{\mathrm{EH}}\,R
          \;+\; c_F\,\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu})
        \Bigr]\sqrt{g}\,d^4x
        \;+\;\text{(higher orders)}.
        \label{eq:ren-action}
      \end{equation}
      The coefficients $\Lambda_{\mathrm{ren}}$, $c_{\mathrm{EH}}$ and $c_F$ are renormalized
      matching data; the spectral expansion fixes the \emph{degree of divergence} of each, not
      their finite values (Gravity~\cite{Beau2026h}, Q12~\cite{Beau2026q12}).

      \begin{lemma}[Metric variation of the gauge kinetic term]
        \label{lem:YM-stress}
        With
        \[
          \tau_{\mu\nu}
          \;\equiv\; \mathrm{tr}_\rho\!\left(F_{\mu\rho}F_\nu{}^\rho
            - \tfrac14 g_{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}\right),
        \]
        one has
        \[
          \frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}\frac{\delta}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}
          \int_M \mathrm{tr}_\rho(F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma})\sqrt{g}\,d^4x
          \;=\;
          2\,\tau_{\mu\nu}.
        \]
      \end{lemma}

      \begin{proof}
        $F_{\mu\nu}$ with both indices down is metric-independent, so the metric enters only
        through the two inverse metrics contracting the indices and through the volume element:
        $\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F^2) = g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}\,\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F_{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma})$.
        Hence
        $\delta\,\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F^2)/\delta g^{\mu\nu} = 2\,\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F_{\mu\rho}F_\nu{}^\rho)$
        and $\delta\sqrt{g}/\delta g^{\mu\nu} = -\tfrac12\sqrt{g}\,g_{\mu\nu}$, so
        \[
          \frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}\frac{\delta}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}
          \bigl[\sqrt{g}\,\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F^2)\bigr]
          = 2\,\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F_{\mu\rho}F_\nu{}^\rho)
            - \tfrac12 g_{\mu\nu}\,\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F^2)
          = 2\,\tau_{\mu\nu}. \qedhere
        \]
      \end{proof}

    \subsection{Gauge-field variation}
      \label{ssec:gauge-var}

      Varying the matched action~\eqref{eq:ren-action} with respect to the admissible connection
      $A$ \emph{at fixed metric}, as in Theorem~1 of Q12~\cite{Beau2026q12}, gives
      \[
        \frac{\delta S^{\mathrm{ren}}}{\delta A^a_\nu}
        \;=\;
        -\,4\,c_F\,I_\rho\,D_\mu F^{a\mu\nu}\sqrt{g}
        \;+\;
        O(F^3,\,R\cdot F),
      \]
      where $D_\mu = \partial_\mu + [A_\mu,\,\cdot\,]$ is the gauge-covariant derivative and
      $I_\rho$ is the Dynkin index of $\rho$.
      Since the overall constant is non-zero, the source-free Yang--Mills equations
      $D_\mu F^{a\mu\nu} = 0$ follow at leading order, independently of the value of $c_F$.

    \subsection{The joint system}
      \label{ssec:joint}

      \begin{theorem}[Conditional Einstein--Yang--Mills system]
        \label{thm:EYM}
        Let $S^{\mathrm{ren}}[g,A]$ be the matched local action~\eqref{eq:ren-action}, and adopt
        the stress-tensor convention
        \[
          T^{\mathrm{YM}}_{\mu\nu}
          \;\equiv\; -\frac{2}{\sqrt{g}}\,
          \frac{\delta}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}
          \int c_F\,\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F^2)\,\sqrt{g}\,d^4x
          \;=\; -4c_F\,\tau_{\mu\nu}.
        \]
        Then joint stationarity $\delta_{g,A}S^{\mathrm{ren}} = 0$ is equivalent to
        \begin{align}
          G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}\,g_{\mu\nu}
          &= 8\pi G_N\,T^{\mathrm{YM}}_{\mu\nu},
          \label{eq:Einstein-YM}
          \\[4pt]
          D_\mu F^{a\mu\nu}
          &= 0,
          \label{eq:YM}
        \end{align}
        where the canonical matching conventions are taken component-wise. Since
        $\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}) = I_\rho\,F^a_{\mu\nu}F^{a\,\mu\nu}$ with
        $I_\rho$ the Dynkin index of $\rho$, matching the canonically normalized gauge kinetic
        term fixes the \emph{product}
        \[
          c_{\mathrm{EH}} \;=\; \frac{1}{16\pi G_N},
          \qquad
          c_F\,I_\rho \;=\; \frac{1}{4g_{\mathrm{YM}}^2},
        \]
        so that $8\pi G_N = 1/(2c_{\mathrm{EH}})$ and
        $\Lambda_{\mathrm{eff}} = \Lambda_{\mathrm{ren}}/(2c_{\mathrm{EH}})$.
        The system is \emph{conditional}: it holds given the matched coefficients, which are
        renormalization data supplied from outside the expansion.
      \end{theorem}

      \begin{remark}[Signature and sign conventions]
        \label{rem:signature}
        The derivation is Euclidean, and $T^{\mathrm{YM}}_{\mu\nu}$ is \emph{defined} above by
        the stated variational convention, $T^{\mathrm{YM}}_{\mu\nu} = -4c_F\tau_{\mu\nu}$.
        Equation~\eqref{eq:Einstein-YM} therefore has the form of the Einstein--Yang--Mills
        system in that convention; identifying it with the Lorentzian Einstein--Yang--Mills
        equations of physics requires a continuation to Lorentzian signature, under which the
        relative sign between the curvature and source terms and the positivity of the
        Yang--Mills energy density must be re-derived rather than assumed.
        That continuation is not carried out here (Section~\ref{ssec:open}).
      \end{remark}

      \begin{proof}
        Vary~\eqref{eq:ren-action} term by term.
        The Einstein--Hilbert term gives $c_{\mathrm{EH}}G_{\mu\nu}\sqrt{g}$;
        the $a_0$ term gives $\tfrac12\Lambda_{\mathrm{ren}}g_{\mu\nu}\sqrt{g}$;
        and by Lemma~\ref{lem:YM-stress} the gauge term gives
        $2c_F\tau_{\mu\nu}\sqrt{g}$.
        Stationarity therefore reads
        \[
          c_{\mathrm{EH}}\,G_{\mu\nu}
          + \tfrac12\Lambda_{\mathrm{ren}}\,g_{\mu\nu}
          + 2c_F\,\tau_{\mu\nu} \;=\; 0 .
        \]
        Substituting $\tau_{\mu\nu} = -T^{\mathrm{YM}}_{\mu\nu}/(4c_F)$ and dividing by
        $c_{\mathrm{EH}}$ gives
        $G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda_{\mathrm{ren}}g_{\mu\nu}/(2c_{\mathrm{EH}})
         = T^{\mathrm{YM}}_{\mu\nu}/(2c_{\mathrm{EH}})$, which is~\eqref{eq:Einstein-YM}
        under the stated matching.
        Equation~\eqref{eq:YM} is the fixed-metric connection variation of
        Section~\ref{ssec:gauge-var}.
      \end{proof}

      \begin{remark}[The normalisation is matching, not prediction]
        \label{rem:no-prediction}
        Equation~\eqref{eq:Einstein-YM} holds \emph{by definition} of
        $T^{\mathrm{YM}}_{\mu\nu}$, whose normalisation already contains $g_{\mathrm{YM}}^{-2}$
        through $c_F I_\rho$; the gravitational coupling enters only through
        $8\pi G_N = 1/(2c_{\mathrm{EH}})$, that is, through the Einstein coefficient alone.
        Nothing in the construction predicts either constant: $G_N$ and $g_{\mathrm{YM}}$ are two
        \emph{independent} renormalized matching data, in accordance with
        Gravity~\cite{Beau2026h} and Q12~\cite{Beau2026q12}.
        In the proper-time scheme of~\eqref{eq:SDW-expansion} the spectral expansion fixes the
        degree of ultraviolet divergence of each coefficient, and nothing more.
      \end{remark}

  \section{The \texorpdfstring{$a_6$}{a6} sector: a cautious inventory}
    \label{sec:a6}

    \subsection{Divergence degree of \texorpdfstring{$a_6$}{a6} in four dimensions}
      \label{ssec:a6-degree}

      In the convention~\eqref{eq:heat-kernel}, the $a_6$ coefficient enters with a positive power
      of the proper time. In four dimensions it therefore produces no ultraviolet divergence: the
      logarithm sits at $a_4$, and in the proper-time scheme of~\eqref{eq:SDW-expansion} the
      $a_6$ sector appears only inside the cutoff-dependent remainder $O(\Lambda^{-2})$.

      This must not be confused with the size of the corresponding term in the effective action.
      The remainder that is $O(\Lambda^{-2})$ is the \emph{cutoff-dependent} part; the
      renormalized local coefficient of each dimension-six operator survives the removal of the
      cutoff and is an independent matching datum, on the same footing as $c_{\mathrm{EH}}$ and
      $c_F$. In particular it is not determined by, and must not be identified with,
      $\ell_{\mathrm{sp}}^2$.

    \subsection{Which invariants can occur}
      \label{ssec:a6-inventory}

      By dimension and derivative counting, $a_6$ collects local invariants of total derivative
      order six~\cite{Vassilevich2003,ParkerToms2009}. Writing $\Omega^{\mathrm{gauge}} = F$ for
      the gauge component of the bundle curvature, a representative spanning list of the
      gauge-dependent terms that can appear --- understood modulo integrations by parts and
      Bianchi identities, and besides the purely gravitational cubic-curvature and $E$-dependent
      invariants --- is:
      \begin{align}
        &R\,\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}),
        \qquad
        R_{\mu\nu}\,\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F^{\mu\rho}F^{\nu}{}_{\rho}),
        \qquad
        R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\,\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F^{\mu\nu}F^{\rho\sigma}),
        \nonumber\\[2pt]
        &\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F_{\mu}{}^{\nu}F_{\nu}{}^{\rho}F_{\rho}{}^{\mu}),
        \qquad
        \mathrm{tr}_\rho(D_\mu F_{\nu\rho}\,D^\mu F^{\nu\rho}),
        \qquad
        \mathrm{tr}_\rho(D_\mu F^{\mu\nu}\,D^\rho F_{\rho\nu}),
        \label{eq:a6-inventory}
      \end{align}
      This list is \emph{not} a basis: the derivative terms are related to the $R\,F^2$-type and
      $F^3$ terms by integration by parts and the Bianchi identities, so the entries
      of~\eqref{eq:a6-inventory} are not mutually independent, and reducing them to a minimal
      independent set is part of the computation not performed here.
      Two structural points nevertheless follow.
      \begin{enumerate}
        \item The pure-gauge invariant at this order is \emph{cubic},
        $\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F^3)$. A quartic term $\mathrm{tr}_\rho(\Omega^4)$ has derivative order
        eight and belongs to $a_8$, not to $a_6$.
        \item There is no single ``leading cross-coupling''. Several mixed structures occur and
        are interrelated by the above identities, so none of them can be singled out as
        \emph{the} gauge--gravity cross-term on the strength of the present analysis.
      \end{enumerate}

    \subsection{What is not determined here}
      \label{ssec:a6-open}

      We do \emph{not} compute the coefficients in~\eqref{eq:a6-inventory} for the admissible
      operator: they are conditional Wilson coefficients, to be fixed either by an explicit
      Seeley--DeWitt computation for $\mathcal{A}_{g,A}$ or by matching. Consequently no $a_6$
      correction to the equations of motion is asserted in this paper.

      \begin{remark}[Form of a mixed contribution to the connection equation]
        \label{rem:no-commutator}
        A mixed $a_6$ contribution to the Yang--Mills equation cannot take a commutator form
        $[R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma},F_{\rho\sigma}]$: the Riemann tensor is a scalar in the gauge
        algebra --- it multiplies the identity on the gauge factor of the bundle --- so any such
        commutator vanishes identically.
        A genuine mixed contribution takes instead the form
        $\nabla_\mu\!\left(R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}F_{\rho\sigma}\right)$ and similar
        curvature-weighted divergences, with coefficients not determined here.
      \end{remark}

  \section{Epistemic status}

    \subsection{Status of the supplied gauge group}
      \label{ssec:Hcolor}

      $G_\Pi$ is a supplied input, and every result of this paper is independent of which group is
      supplied: it enters only through the representation $\rho$ in which the trace
      $\mathrm{tr}_\rho$ and the Dynkin index $I_\rho$ are taken.

      No identification of $G_\Pi$ with a specific compact group is available.
      O31~\cite{Beau2026a35} is a withdrawal notice.
      Among the results it withdraws are the co-admissibility of the individual capacity profiles
      $\sigma_{c_1}$, $\sigma_{c_2}$, $\sigma_{c_3}$, which is the content of
      $[H\text{-color}]_{\mathrm{pointwise}}$ and of its Proposition~4.23; the uniqueness of
      $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ among connected compact subgroups acting irreducibly on $\mathbb{C}^3$;
      the irreducibility of the group of fibre-preserving transformations of the colour eigenspace;
      the arithmetic criterion $q \equiv 1 \pmod 3$; and every derivation of a gauge factor, the
      composite $\mathrm{SU}(3) \times \mathrm{SU}(2) \times \mathrm{U}(1)$, the
      determinant-one condition, the commutation of the putative factors and the direct-product
      structure included.
      Three counterexamples are given there: $\mathrm{SO}(3) \subset \mathrm{SU}(3)$, a proper
      connected compact subgroup acting irreducibly on $\mathbb{C}^3$ with determinant one; the
      diagonal torus $T^2$, which satisfies every constraint that was imposed while acting
      reducibly; and $\{1, 2, q-3\}$, an admissible triplet for every prime $q \geq 7$
      irrespective of $q \bmod 3$.
      Nothing withdrawn there is replaced by a weaker positive statement.

      O32~\cite{Beau2026a36} measures the finite-$q$ residual variance of the capacity profiles.
      Those are measurements; this paper takes no position on their interpretation and does not
      read them as identifying a group.

      Q12~\cite{Beau2026q12} likewise carries $G_\Pi$ as a supplied compact group, and
      Q6a~\cite{Beau2026q6a} treats $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ as a structurally motivated candidate whose
      derivation remains open.
      The identification of $G_\Pi$ is therefore an open question of the gauge-structure
      sub-programme, and it is used nowhere in this paper.

    \subsection{Open points}
      \label{ssec:open}

      The following open points are carried forward:
      \begin{enumerate}
        \item \textbf{Non-linear completion.}
        Whether the admissibility saturation bound $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$~\cite{Beau2026c} induces a
        well-defined non-linear completion of the joint functional is open. Any such construction
        must be checked for dimensional homogeneity of its matrix argument and for the
        semidefinite sign structure of $F_{\mu\rho}F^{\rho}{}_{\nu}$, both of which constrain the
        admissible determinant forms severely.

        \item \textbf{Quantitative $a_6$ coefficients.}
        The coefficients of the invariants listed in~\eqref{eq:a6-inventory} are not computed
        here; they require an explicit Seeley--DeWitt computation for the admissible operator
        $\mathcal{A}_{g,A}$, or independent matching. Until then no $a_6$ correction to the
        equations of motion is asserted.

        \item \textbf{Matching data and the coupling ratio.}
        $G_N$, $g_{\mathrm{YM}}$, $\Lambda_{\mathrm{ren}}$ and the dimension-six coefficients are
        supplied from outside the expansion. Whether the admissibility structure constrains any
        combination of them --- in particular the ratio $G_N g_{\mathrm{YM}}^2$ --- is open; the
        present paper establishes only that the spectral expansion does not determine them.

        \item \textbf{Lorentzian continuation.}
        All spectral derivations are performed in Euclidean signature, and
        $T^{\mathrm{YM}}_{\mu\nu}$ is defined by the variational convention of
        Theorem~\ref{thm:EYM}.
        The Lorentzian continuation is not established in the geometric sector either.
        Under the spatial-limit hypothesis [H-L] (not established) and the open lifting hypothesis [H-lift],
        Q5b~\cite{Beau2026q5b} gives a rank-three co-metric; in homogeneous and quasi-isotropic regimes, and under
        the hyperbolicity hypothesis [H-hyp] of~\cite{Beau2026c} applied to the reduced operator
        $L_{\mathrm{red}}$ of Q5b, the non-degenerate block has signature $(-,+,+)$, with stipulated signs.
        A four-dimensional Lorentzian metric requires a supplied extension~\cite{Beau2026q6b}, and a Lorentzian
        counterpart of the spectral entropy functional $S_\Pi$ requires an in-in construction that is
        open~\cite{Beau2026i}.
        The continuation of the gauge sector, including the relative sign between curvature and source and the
        positivity of the gauge energy density, is a further open step.

        \item \textbf{Internal structure of a non-abelian sector.}
        Theorem~\ref{thm:EYM} holds for any supplied compact $G_\Pi$ and says nothing about the
        internal structure of a particular non-abelian sector --- its colour charge structure or
        its structure constants --- nor about any principle that would single such a sector out.
        No such derivation is available in the programme, and none is claimed here.
      \end{enumerate}

  \section{Conclusion}
    \label{sec:conclusion}

    The robust result of this paper is the \emph{conditional} Einstein--Yang--Mills
    system~\eqref{eq:Einstein-YM}--\eqref{eq:YM}, obtained by varying a single renormalized local
    action with respect to the metric and to the connection.

    \paragraph{What is established.}
      One matched local action supplies both equations: the fixed-metric connection variation
      gives $D_\mu F^{a\mu\nu} = 0$, and the metric variation of the same
      $\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F^2)$ term gives its energy--momentum tensor, via
      $\delta\!\int\!\sqrt{g}\,\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F^2)/\delta g^{\mu\nu} = 2\sqrt{g}\,\tau_{\mu\nu}$.
      Within the proper-time scheme of~\eqref{eq:SDW-expansion}, the gravitational and gauge
      sectors sit at different orders of the heat-kernel expansion and therefore carry different
      degrees of ultraviolet divergence, quadratic at $a_2$ and logarithmic at $a_4$.

    \paragraph{What the paper does not determine.}
      The normalisation of~\eqref{eq:Einstein-YM} is matching, not prediction: with
      $c_{\mathrm{EH}} = 1/(16\pi G_N)$ and $c_F I_\rho = 1/(4g_{\mathrm{YM}}^2)$ the equation
      holds by definition of $T^{\mathrm{YM}}$, and $8\pi G_N = 1/(2c_{\mathrm{EH}})$ depends on
      the Einstein coefficient alone (Remark~\ref{rem:no-prediction}).
      $G_N$, $g_{\mathrm{YM}}$, $\Lambda_{\mathrm{ren}}$ and the dimension-six coefficients remain
      independent renormalized data, and the ratio $G_N g_{\mathrm{YM}}^2$ is therefore not an
      output of this construction.
      The result is Euclidean (Remark~\ref{rem:signature}), and no non-linear completion of the
      joint functional is proposed.

    \paragraph{Interpretive outlook.}
      Beyond these individually labelled results, the reading suggested by the trilogy
      (Gravity~\cite{Beau2026h}, Q12~\cite{Beau2026q12}, Q13) is that gravity and gauge dynamics
      are not unified by enlarging the symmetry group or adding fields, but are \emph{stratified}
      by the Seeley--DeWitt level at which the same operator $\mathcal{A}_{g,A}$ responds to
      variation --- replacing ``what symmetry unifies gravity and gauge?'' with ``at what
      spectral order does the projection respond?''.
      This is an interpretive outlook, not a theorem: it rests on the qualitative separation of
      spectral orders, and not on any quantitative claim about the couplings.
      The natural continuation of the programme is the treatment of fermionic matter and
      chirality.
