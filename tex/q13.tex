  \begin{abstract}
    The companion Gravity and Q12 papers derived, respectively, the Einstein tensor as the
    horizontal $a_2$ response and the local Yang--Mills equations as the vertical $a_4$ response
    of the projective spectral entropy functional
    $S_\Pi[g,A] = \tfrac{1}{2}\log\det' \mathcal{A}_{g,A}$.
    The present paper joins the two variations within a single renormalized local action.
    Its robust content is a \emph{conditional} Einstein--Yang--Mills system: varying the common
    matched action with respect to the connection at fixed metric gives $D_\mu F^{a\mu\nu} = 0$,
    while varying the same $\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F^2)$ term with respect to the metric gives exactly
    the Yang--Mills stress tensor, through
    $(1/\sqrt{g})\,\delta \!\int\!\sqrt{g}\,\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F^2)/\delta g^{\mu\nu}
     = 2\tau_{\mu\nu}$ with
    $\tau_{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{tr}_\rho(F_{\mu\rho}F_\nu{}^\rho - \tfrac14 g_{\mu\nu}F^2)$.
    Once the two Wilson coefficients are matched canonically,
    $c_{\mathrm{EH}} = 1/(16\pi G_N)$ and $c_F = 1/(4g_{\mathrm{YM}}^2)$, the standard equation
    $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G_N T^{\mathrm{YM}}_{\mu\nu}$ is recovered \emph{by definition} of
    $T^{\mathrm{YM}}$. Accordingly $c_{\mathrm{YM}}/c_{\mathrm{EH}}$ is not $8\pi G_N$ and is not
    a prediction: $G_N$ and $g_{\mathrm{YM}}$ remain two independent renormalized matching data.
    The cosmological $a_0$ term does contribute to the metric equation and is retained.
    The $a_6$ sector is given only as a cautious inventory: in four dimensions it is not
    logarithmically divergent, it contains $\Omega^3$ (not $\Omega^4$, which belongs to $a_8$)
    together with several independent $R\,F^2$-type and derivative invariants, so no unique
    cross-term is claimed and its Wilson coefficients are left explicitly conditional.
    The Eddington--Born--Infeld section of the previous version is withdrawn: its determinant
    construction was dimensionally and algebraically invalid, and no replacement is asserted here.
    No numerical gauge--gravity hierarchy is claimed; what is structural is only the qualitative
    difference in ultraviolet divergence degree, quadratic at $a_2$ and logarithmic at $a_4$.
    The gauge group $G_\Pi$ is taken as input from the spectral admissibility sub-programme.
    \emph{Interpretive status.} The reading of gravity and gauge dynamics as \emph{stratified by
    Seeley--DeWitt order} rather than unified by an enlarged symmetry group is offered as an
    interpretive outlook, not a theorem.
  \end{abstract}

  \textbf{Keywords:} gauge--gravity synthesis, Einstein--Yang--Mills equations,

    \subsection{Context}
      \label{ssec:context}

      The Cosmochrony framework derives emergent physics from a generally non-injective
      projection $\Pi\colon\chi\to\mathcal{M}$ of a static relational substrate $\chi$.
      Within this framework, the Q-series of papers establishes the emergence of
      quantum structure, spacetime geometry, and gauge dynamics from the admissibility
      structure of $\Pi$~\cite{Beau2026m,Beau2026q1,Beau2026q2}.
      The spectral entropy functional
      \[
        S_\Pi[g] = \tfrac{1}{2}\log\det'\ \mathcal{A}_g,
      \]
      where $\mathcal{A}_g = -(\nabla^g)^2$ is the admissible Laplacian, encodes
      the effective geometric dynamics of the projection.
      Its metric variation produces the Einstein tensor as the infrared-dominant
      local response via the $a_2$ Seeley--DeWitt coefficient~\cite{Beau2026h}.

      The Q12 paper~\cite{Beau2026q12} extended the functional to the gauge sector
      by coupling $\mathcal{A}_g$ to an admissible connection $A$ on the associated
      vector bundle:
      \[
        S_\Pi[g,A] = \tfrac{1}{2}\log\det'\ \mathcal{A}_{g,A},
        \qquad
        \mathcal{A}_{g,A} = -(\nabla^A)^2 + E.
      \]
      The vertical variation $\delta_A S_\Pi = 0$ then yields the Yang--Mills equations
      $D_\mu F^{a\mu\nu} = 0$ through the $a_4$ coefficient.
      The two results place gravity and Yang--Mills dynamics at different levels of
      the Seeley--DeWitt hierarchy of the \emph{same} functional, separated by geometric
      direction (horizontal versus vertical variation) and by spectral order.

    \subsection{Main results}
      \label{ssec:main}

      The present paper delivers the following, and explicitly withdraws two claims made in
      earlier versions:

      \begin{enumerate}
        \item \textbf{Conditional joint variational problem.}
        We vary a single renormalized local action with respect to both $g$ and $A$ and obtain
        the coupled Einstein--Yang--Mills system, with the Yang--Mills stress tensor arising from
        the metric dependence of the matched $\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F^2)$ term
        (Theorem~\ref{thm:EYM}). The normalisation is fixed by canonical matching, not predicted:
        $G_N$ and $g_{\mathrm{YM}}$ are independent renormalized data
        (Remark~\ref{rem:no-prediction}).

        \item \textbf{Cautious $a_6$ inventory.}
        We list the independent gauge--gravity invariants that can occur at order $a_6$ and
        record what is \emph{not} determined by the present analysis: in four dimensions $a_6$ is
        not logarithmically divergent, and no unique cross-term is computed here
        (Section~\ref{sec:a6}).

        \item \textbf{Withdrawal of the Eddington--Born--Infeld theorem.}
        The EBI construction of the previous version is withdrawn as dimensionally and
        algebraically invalid; no replacement is asserted (Section~\ref{sec:EBI}).

        \item \textbf{Withdrawal of the quantitative hierarchy.}
        The numerical bound previously claimed for $G_N g^2_{YM}$ is withdrawn: both couplings
        are matching data, so their ratio is not an output of the construction
        (Section~\ref{sec:hierarchy}).
      \end{enumerate}

  \section{The joint spectral entropy functional}
    \label{sec:joint}

    \subsection{The admissible principal bundle}
      \label{ssec:bundle}

      Following Q12~\cite{Beau2026q12}, let $(M,g)$ be a compact Riemannian four-manifold
      and let $P\to M$ be a principal $G_\Pi$-bundle, where $G_\Pi$ is the admissible gauge
      group inherited from the spectral admissibility sub-programme~\cite{Beau2026q6a}.
      Let $E = P\times_\rho V$ be the associated vector bundle for a representation
      $\rho\colon G_\Pi\to \mathrm{GL}(V)$.
      An admissible connection $A\in\mathcal{A}(P)$ defines a covariant derivative
      $\nabla^A$ on $E$ and a curvature two-form $F_A = dA + A\wedge A\in\Omega^2(M,\mathrm{ad}\,P)$.

    \subsection{The Laplace-type operator and its decomposition}
      \label{ssec:Laplace}

      Define the Laplace-type operator on sections of $E$:
      \[
        \mathcal{A}_{g,A} = -(\nabla^A)^2 + E,
      \]
      where $E\in\Gamma(\mathrm{End}\,E)$ is the endomorphism determined by the admissibility
      structure.
      Lemma~1 of Q12~\cite{Beau2026q12}, in its corrected form, states only that under minimal
      coupling the coefficient $a_2$ is independent of $A$, so that the \emph{vertical} variation
      at fixed metric isolates the Yang--Mills sector.
      It does \emph{not} assert a full horizontal--vertical decoupling: the metric variation of
      the $a_4$ gauge term is precisely the Yang--Mills stress tensor, an $a_4$-order
      back-reaction which is the subject of Section~\ref{sec:EYM}.
      Accordingly we do not use any operator split of the form
      $\mathcal{A}_g^{\mathrm{hor}}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_V
      + \mathrm{Id}_{\mathcal{H}}\otimes\mathcal{A}_A^{\mathrm{vert}}
      + \mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{mix}}$; such a split was assumed in earlier versions of this paper
      and is withdrawn.
      The spectral entropy functional is then:
      \[
        S_\Pi[g,A] = \tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{Tr}\log\mathcal{A}_{g,A}
        \;=\;
        \tfrac{1}{2}\log\det'\,\mathcal{A}_{g,A}.
      \]
      This is the common generating functional for both the gravitational and gauge sectors.

    \subsection{Seeley--DeWitt expansion of the joint functional}
      \label{ssec:SDW}

      The heat-kernel expansion of $\mathcal{A}_{g,A}$ gives~\cite{Vassilevich2003,ParkerToms2009}:
      \begin{equation}
        \mathrm{Tr}\,e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{g,A}}
        \;\sim\;
        \sum_{k=0}^\infty a_{2k}[\mathcal{A}_{g,A}]\,t^{(2k-4)/2}
        \quad (t\to 0^+),
        \label{eq:heat-kernel}
      \end{equation}
      where the Seeley--DeWitt coefficients $a_{2k}$ are local integrals of
      $g$, $A$, and their covariant derivatives.
      After renormalization of the UV-divergent terms, the functional decomposes as:
      \begin{align}
        S_\Pi[g,A]
        &= c_{(0)}\Lambda^4 a_0
        \;+\;
        c_{(2)}\Lambda^2 a_2[g]
        \;+\;
        c_{(4)}\log\frac{\Lambda}{\mu}\,a_4[g,A]
        \;+\;
        S_\Pi^{\mathrm{fin}}[g,A]
        \;+\;
        O\!\left(\frac{1}{\Lambda^2}\right),
        \label{eq:SDW-expansion}
      \end{align}
      where $S_\Pi^{\mathrm{fin}}$ is the finite part at the renormalization scale $\mu$.
      The $a_0$ sector is \emph{not} inert. With
      $a_0 = \int_M \mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{Id}_V)\sqrt{g}\,d^4x$ and
      $\delta\sqrt{g}/\delta g^{\mu\nu} = -\tfrac12\sqrt{g}\,g_{\mu\nu}$, it contributes a
      cosmological term proportional to $g_{\mu\nu}$ in the metric equation.
      (Earlier versions of this paper asserted that $a_0$ does not contribute to the equations of
      motion; that assertion was incorrect and is withdrawn.)
      Its renormalized coefficient is a further matching datum, written $\Lambda_{\mathrm{ren}}$
      below, and is not predicted here.
      The remaining sectors used in this paper are:
      \begin{align}
        a_2[g]
        &= \frac{1}{16\pi^2}\int_M \frac{R}{6}\,\mathrm{tr}(\mathrm{Id}_V)\,\sqrt{g}\,d^4x,
        \label{eq:a2}
        \\[4pt]
        a_4[g,A]
        &= \frac{1}{16\pi^2}\int_M
        \Bigl[
          \alpha_1 R^2 + \alpha_2 R_{\mu\nu}R^{\mu\nu}
          + \alpha_3 R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
          + \tfrac{1}{12}\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu})
          \Bigr]\sqrt{g}\,d^4x,
        \label{eq:a4}
      \end{align}
      where $\mathrm{tr}_\rho$ is the trace in the fibre representation $\rho$, the gauge
      component $\Omega^{\mathrm{gauge}}_{\mu\nu} = F_{\mu\nu}$ has been isolated from the total
      bundle curvature as in Q12~\cite{Beau2026q12} (the total $\Omega$ also carries the
      spin-connection curvature, which feeds the purely gravitational invariants), and
      $\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3$ are numerical coefficients~\cite{Vassilevich2003}.
      The term $\tfrac{1}{12}\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F^2)$ is the unique gauge-invariant $a_4$
      contribution quadratic in the gauge curvature; its normalisation depends on the
      representation through the Dynkin index $I_\rho$ and is therefore not universal.

  \section{Joint Einstein--Yang--Mills equations of motion}
    \label{sec:EYM}

    \subsection{Metric variation of the joint functional}
      \label{ssec:metric-var}

      We work throughout with a single renormalized local action, in which each surviving
      invariant carries its own Wilson coefficient fixed by matching:
      \begin{equation}
        S^{\mathrm{ren}}[g,A]
        \;=\;
        \int_M \Bigl[
          -\,\Lambda_{\mathrm{ren}}
          \;+\; c_{\mathrm{EH}}\,R
          \;+\; c_F\,\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu})
        \Bigr]\sqrt{g}\,d^4x
        \;+\;\text{(higher orders)}.
        \label{eq:ren-action}
      \end{equation}
      The coefficients $\Lambda_{\mathrm{ren}}$, $c_{\mathrm{EH}}$ and $c_F$ are renormalized
      matching data; the spectral expansion fixes the \emph{degree of divergence} of each, not
      their finite values (Gravity~3.3.1~\cite{Beau2026h}, Q12~1.3~\cite{Beau2026q12}).

      \begin{lemma}[Metric variation of the gauge kinetic term]
        \label{lem:YM-stress}
        With
        \[
          \tau_{\mu\nu}
          \;\equiv\; \mathrm{tr}_\rho\!\left(F_{\mu\rho}F_\nu{}^\rho
            - \tfrac14 g_{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}\right),
        \]
        one has
        \[
          \frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}\frac{\delta}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}
          \int_M \mathrm{tr}_\rho(F_{\rho\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma})\sqrt{g}\,d^4x
          \;=\;
          2\,\tau_{\mu\nu}.
        \]
      \end{lemma}

      \begin{proof}
        $F_{\mu\nu}$ with both indices down is metric-independent, so the metric enters only
        through the two inverse metrics contracting the indices and through the volume element:
        $\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F^2) = g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma}\,\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F_{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma})$.
        Hence
        $\delta\,\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F^2)/\delta g^{\mu\nu} = 2\,\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F_{\mu\rho}F_\nu{}^\rho)$
        and $\delta\sqrt{g}/\delta g^{\mu\nu} = -\tfrac12\sqrt{g}\,g_{\mu\nu}$, so
        \[
          \frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}\frac{\delta}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}
          \bigl[\sqrt{g}\,\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F^2)\bigr]
          = 2\,\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F_{\mu\rho}F_\nu{}^\rho)
            - \tfrac12 g_{\mu\nu}\,\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F^2)
          = 2\,\tau_{\mu\nu}. \qedhere
        \]
      \end{proof}

      \begin{remark}[Correction of the previous normalisation]
        \label{rem:factor}
        Earlier versions of this paper displayed
        $-4\,\mathrm{Tr}(F_{\mu\rho}F_\nu{}^\rho) + g_{\mu\nu}\mathrm{Tr}(F^2)$
        for this variation, i.e.\ $-2\tau_{\mu\nu}$. That expression was wrong by a factor
        $-2$; the correct result is $2\tau_{\mu\nu}$ as above.
      \end{remark}

    \subsection{Gauge-field variation}
      \label{ssec:gauge-var}

      Varying the matched action~\eqref{eq:ren-action} with respect to the admissible connection
      $A$ \emph{at fixed metric}, as in Theorem~1 of Q12~\cite{Beau2026q12}, gives
      \[
        \frac{\delta S^{\mathrm{ren}}}{\delta A^a_\nu}
        \;=\;
        -\,4\,c_F\,I_\rho\,D_\mu F^{a\mu\nu}\sqrt{g}
        \;+\;
        O(F^3,\,R\cdot F),
      \]
      where $D_\mu = \partial_\mu + [A_\mu,\,\cdot\,]$ is the gauge-covariant derivative and
      $I_\rho$ is the Dynkin index of $\rho$.
      Since the overall constant is non-zero, the source-free Yang--Mills equations
      $D_\mu F^{a\mu\nu} = 0$ follow at leading order, independently of the value of $c_F$.

    \subsection{The joint system}
      \label{ssec:joint}

      \begin{theorem}[Conditional Einstein--Yang--Mills system]
        \label{thm:EYM}
        Let $S^{\mathrm{ren}}[g,A]$ be the matched local action~\eqref{eq:ren-action}, and adopt
        the stress-tensor convention
        \[
          T^{\mathrm{YM}}_{\mu\nu}
          \;\equiv\; -\frac{2}{\sqrt{g}}\,
          \frac{\delta}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}
          \int c_F\,\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F^2)\,\sqrt{g}\,d^4x
          \;=\; -4c_F\,\tau_{\mu\nu}.
        \]
        Then joint stationarity $\delta_{g,A}S^{\mathrm{ren}} = 0$ is equivalent to
        \begin{align}
          G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda_{\mathrm{eff}}\,g_{\mu\nu}
          &= 8\pi G_N\,T^{\mathrm{YM}}_{\mu\nu},
          \label{eq:Einstein-YM}
          \\[4pt]
          D_\mu F^{a\mu\nu}
          &= 0,
          \label{eq:YM}
        \end{align}
        where, by the canonical matching conventions
        \[
          c_{\mathrm{EH}} \;=\; \frac{1}{16\pi G_N},
          \qquad
          c_F \;=\; \frac{1}{4g_{\mathrm{YM}}^2},
        \]
        one has $8\pi G_N = 1/(2c_{\mathrm{EH}})$ and
        $\Lambda_{\mathrm{eff}} = \Lambda_{\mathrm{ren}}/(2c_{\mathrm{EH}})$.
        The system is \emph{conditional}: it holds given the matched coefficients, which are
        renormalization data supplied from outside the expansion.
      \end{theorem}

      \begin{proof}
        Vary~\eqref{eq:ren-action} term by term.
        The Einstein--Hilbert term gives $c_{\mathrm{EH}}G_{\mu\nu}\sqrt{g}$;
        the $a_0$ term gives $\tfrac12\Lambda_{\mathrm{ren}}g_{\mu\nu}\sqrt{g}$;
        and by Lemma~\ref{lem:YM-stress} the gauge term gives
        $2c_F\tau_{\mu\nu}\sqrt{g}$.
        Stationarity therefore reads
        \[
          c_{\mathrm{EH}}\,G_{\mu\nu}
          + \tfrac12\Lambda_{\mathrm{ren}}\,g_{\mu\nu}
          + 2c_F\,\tau_{\mu\nu} \;=\; 0 .
        \]
        Substituting $\tau_{\mu\nu} = -T^{\mathrm{YM}}_{\mu\nu}/(4c_F)$ and dividing by
        $c_{\mathrm{EH}}$ gives
        $G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda_{\mathrm{ren}}g_{\mu\nu}/(2c_{\mathrm{EH}})
         = T^{\mathrm{YM}}_{\mu\nu}/(2c_{\mathrm{EH}})$, which is~\eqref{eq:Einstein-YM}
        under the stated matching.
        Equation~\eqref{eq:YM} is the fixed-metric connection variation of
        Section~\ref{ssec:gauge-var}.
      \end{proof}

      \begin{remark}[The normalisation is matching, not prediction]
        \label{rem:no-prediction}
        Equation~\eqref{eq:Einstein-YM} is recovered \emph{by definition} of
        $T^{\mathrm{YM}}_{\mu\nu}$, whose normalisation already contains $g_{\mathrm{YM}}^{-2}$
        through $c_F$. In particular
        \[
          8\pi G_N \;=\; \frac{1}{2c_{\mathrm{EH}}}
          \qquad\text{and not}\qquad
          8\pi G_N \;=\; \frac{c_{\mathrm{YM}}}{c_{\mathrm{EH}}},
        \]
        the latter identification --- asserted in earlier versions of this paper --- being
        incorrect, since it mixes the gauge-action prefactor with the definition of the stress
        tensor. Nothing in the construction predicts either constant: $G_N$ and
        $g_{\mathrm{YM}}$ are two \emph{independent} renormalized matching data, in accordance
        with Gravity~3.3.1~\cite{Beau2026h} and Q12~1.3~\cite{Beau2026q12}. The spectral
        expansion fixes only the degree of ultraviolet divergence of each coefficient.
      \end{remark}

  \section{The \texorpdfstring{$a_6$}{a6} sector: a cautious inventory}
    \label{sec:a6}

    \subsection{Divergence degree of \texorpdfstring{$a_6$}{a6} in four dimensions}
      \label{ssec:a6-degree}

      In the normalisation of~\eqref{eq:heat-kernel}, $a_{2k}$ multiplies $t^{(2k-4)/2}$, so in
      four dimensions $a_6$ carries $t^{+1}$.
      It is therefore \emph{not} ultraviolet divergent: the logarithm sits at the $t^0$
      coefficient $a_4$, while $a_6$ contributes a finite, inverse-cutoff-suppressed correction
      of relative order $\ell_{\mathrm{sp}}^2$.
      Earlier versions of this paper attached a factor $\log(\Lambda/\mu)$ to the $a_6$
      corrections; that is incorrect and is withdrawn.

    \subsection{Which invariants can occur}
      \label{ssec:a6-inventory}

      By dimension and derivative counting, $a_6$ collects local invariants of total derivative
      order six~\cite{Vassilevich2003,ParkerToms2009}. Writing $\Omega^{\mathrm{gauge}} = F$ for
      the gauge component of the bundle curvature, the terms that can appear include, besides the
      purely gravitational cubic-curvature and $E$-dependent invariants:
      \begin{align}
        &R\,\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}),
        \qquad
        R_{\mu\nu}\,\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F^{\mu\rho}F^{\nu}{}_{\rho}),
        \qquad
        R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\,\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F^{\mu\nu}F^{\rho\sigma}),
        \nonumber\\[2pt]
        &\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F_{\mu}{}^{\nu}F_{\nu}{}^{\rho}F_{\rho}{}^{\mu}),
        \qquad
        \mathrm{tr}_\rho(D_\mu F_{\nu\rho}\,D^\mu F^{\nu\rho}),
        \qquad
        \mathrm{tr}_\rho(D_\mu F^{\mu\nu}\,D^\rho F_{\rho\nu}),
        \label{eq:a6-inventory}
      \end{align}
      each with its own coefficient. Two corrections to the previous version are recorded:
      \begin{enumerate}
        \item The pure-gauge invariant at this order is \emph{cubic},
        $\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F^3)$, not quartic. A term $\mathrm{tr}_\rho(\Omega^4)$ has derivative
        order eight and belongs to $a_8$, not to $a_6$; its appearance in~$a_6$ in earlier
        versions was an error.
        \item There is no single ``leading cross-coupling''. The three mixed invariants
        in~\eqref{eq:a6-inventory} are independent, and singling out
        $R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F^{\mu\nu}F^{\rho\sigma})$ as \emph{the}
        cross-term is not justified by the present analysis.
      \end{enumerate}

    \subsection{What is not determined here}
      \label{ssec:a6-open}

      We do \emph{not} compute the coefficients in~\eqref{eq:a6-inventory} for the admissible
      operator: they are conditional Wilson coefficients, to be fixed either by an explicit
      Seeley--DeWitt computation for $\mathcal{A}_{g,A}$ or by matching. Consequently no $a_6$
      correction to the equations of motion is asserted in this paper.

      \begin{remark}[An impossible commutator]
        \label{rem:no-commutator}
        Earlier versions displayed an $a_6$ correction to the Yang--Mills equation of the form
        $[R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma},F_{\rho\sigma}]$. This expression is identically zero and cannot
        occur: the Riemann tensor is a scalar in the gauge algebra (it multiplies the identity on
        the gauge factor of the bundle), so it commutes with every Lie-algebra-valued field.
        A genuine mixed contribution to the connection equation takes instead the form
        $\nabla_\mu\!\left(R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}F_{\rho\sigma}\right)$ and similar
        curvature-weighted divergences, with coefficients not determined here.
      \end{remark}

  \section{Withdrawal of the Eddington--Born--Infeld construction}
    \label{sec:EBI}

    The previous version of this paper contained a gauge-sector Born--Infeld Lagrangian, a joint
    Eddington--Born--Infeld functional, and a theorem asserting that the latter recovers the joint
    equations at leading order and is bounded above.
    That construction is \emph{withdrawn}. It fails on several independent counts, each fatal on
    its own:

    \begin{enumerate}
      \item \textbf{The Pfaffian claim is false.} It was asserted that
      $\sqrt{\det(\delta^\mu_\nu + \ell_{\mathrm{sp}}^2 F^\mu{}_\nu)}$ equals the Pfaffian of $F$
      and can change sign in Euclidean signature. For real antisymmetric $F$ the eigenvalues occur
      in pairs $\pm i\lambda_j$, so
      $\det(I + \ell_{\mathrm{sp}}^2 F) = \prod_j\bigl(1+\ell_{\mathrm{sp}}^4\lambda_j^2\bigr)
      \geq 1 > 0$:
      the determinant is manifestly positive, and it is not the Pfaffian of $F$.
      \item \textbf{The proposed replacement has the opposite sign.} The combination
      $M_{\mu\nu} = F_{\mu\rho}F^{\rho}{}_{\nu}$ was described as positive semidefinite. For
      antisymmetric $F$, $M = -FF^{\mathsf{T}}$ is negative semidefinite.
      \item \textbf{The powers of $\ell_{\mathrm{sp}}$ are inconsistent.} $M$ is quadratic in the
      field strength, so a dimensionless matrix argument requires $\ell_{\mathrm{sp}}^4 M$, not
      $\ell_{\mathrm{sp}}^2 M$; the expansion given in the proof changed the power between the
      statement and its conclusion.
      \item \textbf{The joint tensor is dimensionally inhomogeneous.} With
      $\kappa^2 = 8\pi G_N$, the terms $R_{\mu\nu}$ and $\kappa F_{\mu\rho}F^{\rho}{}_{\nu}$ do
      not carry the same dimension, so the sum $R_{\mu\nu} + \kappa F^2_{\mu\nu}$ is not defined.
      \item \textbf{The boundedness property does not follow.} The claimed upper bound was
      derived from $|\det(I+X)| \leq e^{\mathrm{tr}X}$, an inequality valid for positive
      semidefinite $X$ and not applicable here; no fixed upper bound follows, and the asserted
      one is false in general.
    \end{enumerate}

    No replacement completion is asserted in this paper. Whether the admissibility saturation
    bound $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$~\cite{Beau2026c} induces a well-defined non-linear completion of the
    joint functional is left open, to be rebuilt independently if at all.


  \section{Withdrawal of the quantitative hierarchy}
    \label{sec:hierarchy}

    Earlier versions of this paper stated a proposition asserting
    \[
      G_N\,g_{\mathrm{YM}}^2
      \;=\;
      \frac{12\,\ell_{\mathrm{sp}}^2}{\dim V}\cdot\Bigl[\log\frac{\Lambda}{\mu}\Bigr]^{-1}
      \;\ll\;1
    \]
    as a structural output requiring no fine-tuning, on the strength of explicit induced values
    $G_N^{-1} \sim \ell_{\mathrm{sp}}^{-2}$ and
    $g_{\mathrm{YM}}^{-2} \sim \log(\Lambda/\mu)$.
    That proposition is \emph{withdrawn}, together with the explicit coupling values it rests on.

    Two independent reasons make it untenable. First, it contradicts the corrected companion
    results: Gravity~3.3.1~\cite{Beau2026h} establishes that the renormalized Einstein
    coefficient, hence $G_N$, is a matching datum whose value and sign are fixed by
    renormalization conditions rather than predicted, and Q12~1.3~\cite{Beau2026q12} establishes
    the same for $g_{\mathrm{YM}}$, of which only the logarithmic running is calculable and whose
    coefficient is representation-dependent through the Dynkin index $I_\rho$. A ratio of two
    quantities that are each matching data is itself a matching datum, not an output. Second, the
    derivation used the coefficient identification corrected in
    Remark~\ref{rem:no-prediction}.

    What survives is qualitative and scheme-independent: the two sectors sit at different orders
    of the heat-kernel expansion and therefore carry different degrees of ultraviolet divergence,
    \begin{equation}
      a_2 \;\longrightarrow\; \text{gravity, quadratic},
      \qquad
      a_4 \;\longrightarrow\; \text{gauge, logarithmic}.
      \label{eq:divergence-degrees}
    \end{equation}
    This difference in divergence degree is a genuine structural feature of the expansion. It
    does not, by itself, determine the finite renormalized couplings, and no numerical value or
    bound for $G_N g_{\mathrm{YM}}^2$ is claimed here.

    \subsection{Analytical status of [H-color]}
      \label{ssec:Hcolor}

      The identification $G_\Pi = \mathrm{SU}(3)\times\mathrm{SU}(2)\times\mathrm{U}(1)$
      is taken as input from the spectral admissibility sub-programme.
      The $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ sector is established unconditionally for the
      colour-adapted Cayley graph in~\cite{Beau2026a35}.
      For the standard Cayley graph, $[H\text{-color}]_{\mathrm{eff}}$
      (equality of capacity exponents in the large-$q$ limit) is proved
      analytically in~\cite{Beau2026a35,Beau2026a36} via the intra-coset
      Vandermonde mechanism and the modulation-frequency argument (O32).
      Furthermore, $[H\text{-color}]_{\mathrm{pointwise}}$ (exact equality at
      finite~$q$) is proved in O31 (Proposition~4.23,~\cite{Beau2026a35}): each
      O12 fingerprint vector carries a single frequency
      $B = c_1 b_1 + c_2 b_2 + c_3 b_3$, and the orbit dilation
      $B \mapsto \omega B$ is a bijection of $\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z}$, so
      $\sigma_c(n) = \sigma_{\omega c}(n) = \sigma_{\omega^2 c}(n)$ holds exactly.
      All results of the present paper are independent of these results:
      the identification of $G_\Pi$ is an input, and it enters here only through the
      representation $\rho$ in which the trace $\mathrm{tr}_\rho$ and the Dynkin index $I_\rho$
      are taken.

    \subsection{Open points}
      \label{ssec:open}

      The following open points are carried forward:
      \begin{enumerate}
        \item \textbf{Non-linear completion.}
        Whether the admissibility saturation bound $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$~\cite{Beau2026c} induces a
        well-defined non-linear completion of the joint functional is open. The construction
        attempted in earlier versions is withdrawn (Section~\ref{sec:EBI}) and would have to be
        rebuilt from scratch, with dimensional consistency and the sign structure of $F$ checked
        at each step.

        \item \textbf{Quantitative $a_6$ coefficients.}
        The coefficients of the invariants listed in~\eqref{eq:a6-inventory} are not computed
        here; they require an explicit Seeley--DeWitt computation for the admissible operator
        $\mathcal{A}_{g,A}$, or independent matching. Until then no $a_6$ correction to the
        equations of motion is asserted.

        \item \textbf{Matching data.}
        $G_N$, $g_{\mathrm{YM}}$ and $\Lambda_{\mathrm{ren}}$ are supplied from outside the
        expansion. Whether the admissibility structure constrains any combination of them is
        open; the present paper establishes only that the spectral expansion does not determine
        them individually.

        \item \textbf{Lorentzian continuation.}
        All spectral derivations are performed in Euclidean signature.
        The Lorentzian continuation is established at the level of Q5b~\cite{Beau2026q6b} for the
        geometric sector; its extension to the gauge sector remains an open step.

        \item \textbf{$\mathrm{SU}(3)$ Yang--Mills uniqueness.}
        The analogue of Theorem~\ref{thm:EYM} for the internal structure of
        the $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ sector (colour charge structure, structure constants)
        is identified as an open problem in~\cite{Beau2026a35}.
        This is a separate question from $[H\text{-color}]_{\mathrm{eff}}$,
        which is now proved analytically in~\cite{Beau2026a35,Beau2026a36}.
      \end{enumerate}

  \section{Conclusion}
    \label{sec:conclusion}

    The robust result of this paper is the \emph{conditional} Einstein--Yang--Mills
    system~\eqref{eq:Einstein-YM}--\eqref{eq:YM}, obtained by varying a single renormalized local
    action with respect to the metric and to the connection.

    \paragraph{What is established.}
      One matched local action supplies both equations: the fixed-metric connection variation
      gives $D_\mu F^{a\mu\nu} = 0$, and the metric variation of the same
      $\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F^2)$ term gives its energy--momentum tensor, via
      $\delta\!\int\!\sqrt{g}\,\mathrm{tr}_\rho(F^2)/\delta g^{\mu\nu} = 2\sqrt{g}\,\tau_{\mu\nu}$.
      The gravitational and gauge sectors sit at different orders of the heat-kernel expansion
      and therefore carry different degrees of ultraviolet divergence,
      quadratic at $a_2$ and logarithmic at $a_4$~\eqref{eq:divergence-degrees}.

    \paragraph{What is not established.}
      The normalisation of~\eqref{eq:Einstein-YM} is matching, not prediction: after
      $c_{\mathrm{EH}} = 1/(16\pi G_N)$ and $c_F = 1/(4g_{\mathrm{YM}}^2)$ the equation holds by
      definition of $T^{\mathrm{YM}}$, and $8\pi G_N = 1/(2c_{\mathrm{EH}})$ rather than
      $c_{\mathrm{YM}}/c_{\mathrm{EH}}$ (Remark~\ref{rem:no-prediction}).
      $G_N$, $g_{\mathrm{YM}}$ and $\Lambda_{\mathrm{ren}}$ remain independent renormalized data.
      Three claims of earlier versions are withdrawn: the Eddington--Born--Infeld theorem
      (Section~\ref{sec:EBI}), the numerical gauge--gravity hierarchy
      (Section~\ref{sec:hierarchy}), and the identification of a unique logarithmically divergent
      $a_6$ cross-coupling (Section~\ref{sec:a6}).

    \paragraph{Interpretive outlook.}
      Beyond these individually labelled results, the reading suggested by the trilogy
      (Gravity~\cite{Beau2026h}, Q12~\cite{Beau2026q12}, Q13) is that gravity and gauge dynamics
      are not unified by enlarging the symmetry group or adding fields, but are \emph{stratified}
      by the Seeley--DeWitt level at which the same operator $\mathcal{A}_{g,A}$ responds to
      variation --- replacing ``what symmetry unifies gravity and gauge?'' with ``at what
      spectral order does the projection respond?''.
      This is an interpretive outlook, not a theorem: it rests on the qualitative divergence-degree
      separation, which is structural, and not on any of the withdrawn quantitative claims.
      The natural continuation of the programme is the treatment of fermionic matter and
      chirality.
